## Reading Goal

Understand Weinberg 1989 thoroughly as a historical and technical turning point in
nonlinear quantum mechanics.

Do not treat the paper only through the later Polchinski objection. First
understand what Weinberg was trying to build.

## Initial Framing

Weinberg starts from a methodological problem:

> To test quantum mechanics itself, one needs a controlled generalized theory that
> reduces to ordinary quantum mechanics when small parameters vanish.

The analogy is how one tests general relativity or the standard model by embedding
them in larger parameterized frameworks. Weinberg's question is whether quantum
mechanics has a similarly disciplined nonlinear generalization.

## First Concepts To Track

```text
ray-based state:
    psi and Z psi represent the same physical state.

observable:
    ordinary QM: Hermitian operator;
    Weinberg: real homogeneous function of psi and psi*.

homogeneity:
    required so physical predictions depend on the ray, not arbitrary normalization
    or phase.

Hamiltonian dynamics:
    quantum state evolution is written in Hamiltonian form on state space.

nonlinearity:
    arises when the Hamiltonian function is not bilinear/quadratic in psi and psi*.

separated systems:
    the difficult issue of combining noninteracting subsystems without introducing
    unphysical correlations.
```

## Questions For A Slow Reading

```text
1. What features of ordinary quantum mechanics does Weinberg deliberately preserve?
2. What features does he allow to change?
3. Why are observables functions rather than operators?
4. How does the commutator/Poisson-bracket-like structure work?
5. How are symmetries represented?
6. How is time evolution generated?
7. What replaces ordinary eigenvalues and eigenvectors?
8. How does he interpret expectation values and probabilities?
9. Where does the separated-system problem enter?
10. What exactly does the note added in proof concede about Polchinski's objection?
```

## Working Interpretation

Weinberg 1989 is best approached as:

```text
not:
    "quantum mechanics is probably nonlinear"

not:
    "all nonlinear corrections are impossible"

but:
    "here is a mathematically controlled way to ask whether small nonlinear
    corrections to quantum mechanics could exist and be experimentally bounded"
```
