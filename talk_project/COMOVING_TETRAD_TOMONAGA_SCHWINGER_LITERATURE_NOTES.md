# Comoving Tetrads, Tomonaga-Schwinger Foliations, and Related Literature

Purpose: preserve a preliminary literature orientation for the possible Section 6
thread connecting Tomonaga-Schwinger hypersurface evolution, comoving tetrad
frames, accelerated particle worldlines, and foliation/path-independence.

## Working Question

Is there prior published or unpublished work that already develops the specific
idea of using a comoving tetrad frame along an arbitrarily accelerated classical
particle worldline as a local anchor for Tomonaga-Schwinger hypersurface
evolution, then extending that local structure to admissible Cauchy foliations
subject to refoliation/path-independence constraints?

Preliminary answer: there is substantial nearby literature, including some very
close work on accelerated observers and equal-time Cauchy hypersurfaces. I did
not find an obvious prior paper with exactly this synthesis.

The likely novelty is not the existence of accelerated-observer foliations, nor
the Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism, nor the Frenet-Serret description of worldlines
separately. The possible original contribution lies in combining them as a
diagnostic framework:

> Use the comoving tetrad/Frenet-Serret invariants of an accelerated particle
> worldline as the local geometric seed; extend that data to admissible Cauchy
> foliations; treat the extension freedom as auxiliary or gauge-like; and require
> Tomonaga-Schwinger path independence/refoliation independence, especially as a
> test of proposed nonlinear relativistic quantum dynamics.

## Closest Prior Art: Alba and Lusanna

The closest work found is by David Alba and Luca Lusanna:

**Generalized Radar 4-Coordinates and Equal-Time Cauchy Surfaces for Arbitrary
Accelerated Observers**

- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0501090>
- INSPIRE: <https://inspirehep.net/literature/675750>
- Published as: Int. J. Mod. Phys. D 16, 1149-1186 (2007)

This paper constructs observer-dependent, globally defined radar coordinates and
admissible spacelike Cauchy hypersurfaces for arbitrary accelerated observers
using 3+1 splittings of spacetime.

Key relevance:

- It directly addresses the problem that ordinary Fermi coordinates are only
  locally defined.
- It asks how non-inertial observers can build equal-time surfaces that function
  as good Cauchy surfaces.
- It treats clock synchronization conventions and admissible 3+1 splittings as
  observer-dependent structures.
- In parametrized Minkowski theory, different admissible synchronization
  conventions can be treated as gauge equivalent.
- It gives a rigorous setting for the idea that an accelerated observer can be
  associated with a family of spacelike instantaneous 3-spaces.

Why this matters for the present idea:

Alba and Lusanna appear to cover much of the geometric and relativistic
kinematical ground: accelerated observers, radar coordinates, admissible
foliations, and Cauchy hypersurfaces. Any original paper should cite and engage
this work directly.

Where the possible difference remains:

Their work does not appear, at least from the abstract-level review, to make the
specific move of using the comoving Frenet-Serret tetrad of an arbitrarily
accelerated particle as a local anchor for Tomonaga-Schwinger state evolution,
then turning refoliation/path-independence into a constraint on nonlinear or
AI-generated relativistic quantum dynamics.

## Tomonaga-Schwinger Evolution and Arbitrary Cauchy Surfaces

### Torre and Varadarajan

Important cautionary prior:

**Functional Evolution of Free Quantum Fields**

- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9811222>

Torre and Varadarajan study evolution of quantum fields between arbitrary Cauchy
surfaces. They show that for free quantum fields in flat spacetime of dimension
greater than two, evolution between generic Cauchy surfaces generally cannot be
implemented unitarily on the usual Fock space.

This is a serious caveat for any naive statement that the Tomonaga-Schwinger
picture simply gives a well-defined Schrödinger evolution between arbitrary
hypersurfaces.

Implication for the present idea:

If a proposed framework uses states on arbitrary hypersurfaces, it must be clear
whether it is working formally, algebraically, perturbatively, with cutoffs, in
a restricted class of foliations, or in a representation where the Torre-
Varadarajan obstruction is avoided or reinterpreted.

Related work:

**Quantum Fields at Any Time**

- Utah State page: <https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/physics_facpub/35/>

This work treats arbitrary foliations in 1+1-dimensional flat spacetime and
finds better behavior there, while noting that higher-dimensional spacetimes
raise fundamental difficulties.

## Frenet-Serret Worldlines and Accelerated Detectors

### Letaw

Foundational prior:

**Stationary World Lines and the Vacuum Excitation of Noninertial Detectors**

- Phys. Rev. D 23, 1709 (1981)
- DOI page: <https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.23.1709>

Letaw develops generalized Frenet equations in Minkowski spacetime, classifies
stationary worldlines with constant curvature invariants, and studies the
vacuum excitation spectra of noninertial detectors.

Key relevance:

- The Frenet-Serret invariants are used to classify timelike worldlines.
- Curvature corresponds to proper acceleration.
- Torsion and hypertorsion encode additional invariant rotational structure.
- Stationary worldlines are related to timelike Killing vector orbits.
- The work explicitly connects accelerated worldline geometry to quantum field
  detector response.

Why this matters:

Letaw is likely a central citation for the claim that Frenet-Serret invariants
are physically meaningful in QFT contexts, especially where accelerated
detectors or noninertial observers are involved.

### Recent Continuations

Examples of modern continuation of this line:

**Quantum Energy Inequalities Along Stationary Worldlines**

- Christopher J. Fewster and Jacob Thompson, Class. Quantum Grav. 40 (2023)
- Search/result page:
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371916509_Quantum_Energy_Inequalities_along_stationary_worldlines>

This work studies quantum energy inequalities for massless scalar fields along
stationary worldlines and expresses bounds in terms of curvature invariants of
the worldline.

**Unruh-like Effects: Effective Temperatures Along Stationary Worldlines**

- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08225>

This paper studies detector temperatures along stationary, uniformly accelerated
worldlines in Minkowski spacetime, with dependence on curvature, torsion, and
hypertorsion.

Relevance:

These papers reinforce that curvature, torsion, and hypertorsion are not merely
formal geometric ornaments. They can enter physically meaningful QFT quantities
for accelerated observers or detectors.

## Parametrized Minkowski Theory and Hypersurface Gauge Freedom

There is a broader Dirac/ADM/parametrized-field-theory lineage behind the idea
that dynamics can be represented on arbitrary spacelike hypersurfaces.

Relevant conceptual points:

- Dirac's forms of relativistic dynamics already emphasize different choices of
  hypersurface.
- Parametrized Minkowski theories reformulate relativistic systems on arbitrary
  spacelike hypersurfaces.
- The embedding variables describing the hypersurface can often be interpreted
  as gauge variables.
- The Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism is the quantum-field-theoretic descendant of
  this "many-fingered time" idea.

One source encountered in the search states that reformulation of relativistic
isolated systems on arbitrary spacelike hypersurfaces provides the classical
basis of the Tomonaga-Schwinger formulation of quantum field theory:

- <https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=ec87009b5643110ac55c7d34ab29f895ebcb4d51&repid=rep1&type=pdf>

This background strengthens the formulation that arbitrary foliation extension
data should not become physical unless the theory intentionally introduces a
preferred foliation or frame.

## Directness of Prior Art

The literature appears to divide into three nearby but distinct streams:

1. **Tomonaga-Schwinger / arbitrary hypersurface QFT**
   - State evolution between spacelike hypersurfaces.
   - Integrability, microcausality, path independence.
   - Representation-level problems for generic surfaces.

2. **Accelerated observers / radar coordinates / Cauchy surfaces**
   - Fermi normal coordinates, Fermi-Walker frames, noninertial frames.
   - Global radar coordinates and equal-time surfaces for accelerated observers.
   - Clock synchronization and foliation choice as convention or gauge.

3. **Frenet-Serret worldlines / accelerated detector QFT**
   - Curvature, torsion, and hypertorsion as Lorentz-invariant descriptors of
     timelike worldlines.
   - Stationary worldlines and detector response.
   - Unruh-like effects and quantum energy inequalities along accelerated
     trajectories.

The possible paper idea sits at the intersection of these streams. The
intersection seems underdeveloped, at least based on this preliminary search.

## Proposed Novelty Framing

Avoid claiming:

> No one has connected accelerated observers with Cauchy foliations.

That would be too strong. Alba and Lusanna are direct counterexamples.

A safer novelty claim:

> Prior work has developed Tomonaga-Schwinger hypersurface evolution, admissible
> Cauchy foliations for accelerated observers, and Frenet-Serret descriptions of
> accelerated worldlines largely as separate tools. The proposed contribution is
> to use the comoving Frenet-Serret tetrad of an arbitrarily accelerated particle
> as local geometric input for a family of admissible Cauchy foliations, and to
> use Tomonaga-Schwinger path independence as the criterion that removes the
> arbitrary extension freedom from physical predictions.

An even sharper formulation:

> Given a timelike worldline with comoving Frenet-Serret tetrad data
> `(u, e_i; kappa, tau, nu)`, construct local observer-adapted hypersurface
> patches and extend them to admissible Cauchy foliations. Because the extension
> away from the worldline is nonunique, a viable covariant quantum dynamics must
> be invariant under changes of that extension. In Tomonaga-Schwinger language,
> this is precisely a path-independence or refoliation-independence requirement.

## Technical Caveats

1. **Local tetrads do not determine global Cauchy surfaces uniquely.**

   A comoving tetrad along a worldline gives local frame data. Extending that
   data to a full Cauchy hypersurface is a separate global problem and is
   generally nonunique.

2. **Fermi coordinates are local.**

   Ordinary Fermi or Fermi-Walker constructions can break down away from the
   worldline because of caustics or coordinate singularities. Alba/Lusanna-style
   admissible radar-coordinate foliations are relevant precisely because they
   address this limitation.

3. **Tomonaga-Schwinger evolution is not automatically a unitary Fock-space
   Schrödinger evolution for arbitrary surfaces.**

   Torre and Varadarajan show an obstruction for generic Cauchy-surface
   evolution in dimensions greater than two. Any proposed framework must state
   what mathematical setting it assumes.

4. **A preferred foliation would change the interpretation.**

   If a theory intentionally introduces a physical preferred foliation, then
   foliation dependence is not automatically a defect. But if the foliation is
   supposed to be representational, extension dependence must cancel from
   physical predictions.

5. **Nonlinear relativistic quantum dynamics raises additional constraints.**

   In the Hsu/Weinberg/Polchinski context, path independence is not just a
   geometric nicety. It becomes a stringent consistency test: do different
   hypersurface deformations produce the same physical state or observable
   predictions?

## Commentary for Section 6

This thread could be presented as a personal research speculation rather than as
a finished theorem.

The key point is that the comoving tetrad along an accelerated particle is not
merely a coordinate convenience. It packages local Lorentz-invariant information
about the particle's history: acceleration, torsion, and hypertorsion. These are
natural candidates for observer-adapted local data.

But Tomonaga-Schwinger evolution needs states on spacelike hypersurfaces, not
just on a worldline. Therefore the tetrad data must be extended outward into
spacelike Cauchy surfaces. That extension is not unique. The physics must either
depend on the chosen extension, in which case the extension is physical, or be
independent of it, in which case the extension is gauge-like.

This gives a clean conceptual bridge:

```text
accelerated worldline
    -> comoving tetrad / Frenet-Serret invariants
    -> local hypersurface patches
    -> admissible Cauchy foliations
    -> Tomonaga-Schwinger evolution
    -> path-independence/refoliation-independence constraint
```

For the talk, the most defensible statement is:

> There is serious prior work on each component of this chain, especially
> Alba-Lusanna on accelerated observers and Cauchy surfaces, Letaw on
> Frenet-Serret worldlines in QFT detector physics, and Torre-Varadarajan on the
> limits of arbitrary hypersurface evolution. What may be less explored is using
> the comoving tetrad of an accelerated particle as a local seed for
> Tomonaga-Schwinger foliations and then using refoliation independence as a
> diagnostic for proposed nonlinear relativistic quantum dynamics.
