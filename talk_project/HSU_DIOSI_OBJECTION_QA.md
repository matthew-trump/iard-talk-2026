# Hsu / Diosi Objection Q&A

## Context

Lajos Diosi posted a February 2026 arXiv comment on Hsu's *Physics Letters B* paper:

> Lajos Diosi, "Comment on 'Relativistic covariance and nonlinear quantum mechanics:
> Tomonaga-Schwinger analysis'," arXiv:2602.06845.

The comment directly disputes Hsu's central physics claim. It does **not** dispute
the AI-workflow facts: Hsu's companion methodology paper, the GPT-5 exchange, the
Generate-Verify process, or the disclosure gap.

For the talk, the key distinction is:

- Hsu remains a strong anchor case for an AI-originated research direction entering
  peer-reviewed theoretical physics.
- Hsu should not be presented as having settled the nonlinear-QM covariance question.

---

## Brief Summary of Diosi's Objection

Diosi's objection is that Hsu conflates two issues:

1. Whether the nonlinear Tomonaga-Schwinger equation remains integrable and covariant.
2. Whether nonlinear quantum dynamics creates causality or no-signaling problems.

Hsu argues that nonlinear, state-dependent Tomonaga-Schwinger evolution generically
breaks foliation independence, and therefore relativistic covariance.

Diosi says that conclusion is wrong. In his formulation, the nonlinear TS equation
can remain integrable and covariant, even though nonlinear quantum mechanics can still
have acausality problems.

---

## The Technical Core

Diosi rewrites the nonlinear TS equation in the standard interaction-picture language.
In that setup:

- Local fields evolve with the free Hamiltonian.
- The interaction Hamiltonian density depends locally on expectation values.
- The extra state-dependent derivative terms in the integrability condition cancel
  because of microcausality at spacelike separation.

So Diosi's claim is:

> The nonlinear TS equation is still integrable, and therefore still covariant, if
> formulated correctly.

His criticism of Hsu is that Hsu mixes state evolution and field evolution in a way
that Diosi regards as redundant and problematic.

---

## What Diosi Does Not Deny

Diosi does **not** deny that nonlinear quantum dynamics can create superluminal
signaling or acausality problems.

His point is narrower:

- Acausality does not automatically imply failure of TS covariance.
- Local nonlinear dynamics may remain covariant while still being operationally
  problematic once measurements, collapse, and initial entanglement enter.

In his reading, Hsu misidentifies the mechanism of acausality. The problem is not
that local nonlinearity itself generates entanglement or directly violates
microcausality. Rather, nonlinear quantum dynamics can enable signaling through
initially entangled systems plus local measurements and collapse.

---

## Short Answer for Audience Questions

If asked, say:

> Diosi's objection is not that the AI-origin story is false. His objection is to
> the physics conclusion. He argues that Hsu conflates loss of relativistic covariance
> with the familiar causality problems of nonlinear quantum mechanics. In Diosi's
> standard interaction-picture formulation, the Tomonaga-Schwinger integrability
> condition remains satisfied because the relevant extra terms cancel by microcausality.
> So I am not treating Hsu's no-go theorem as settled. For this talk, Hsu is the anchor
> case for a serious AI-originated research direction entering peer-reviewed physics,
> not proof that the nonlinear-QM covariance question is closed.

---

## Slightly Longer Answer

If the audience wants more technical detail:

> Hsu's claim is that state-dependent nonlinear additions to the local Hamiltonian
> density fail the Tomonaga-Schwinger integrability condition, so different spacelike
> foliations give inequivalent evolution. Diosi says this is an artifact of how Hsu
> treats state and field evolution. In the standard interaction-picture TS setup, the
> local fields evolve freely, the interaction Hamiltonian density depends locally on
> the state through expectation values, and the extra derivative terms vanish at
> spacelike separation by microcausality. That preserves integrability and covariance.
> Diosi still accepts that nonlinear QM has acausality problems, but says those arise
> through the Gisin-type measurement/entanglement mechanism, not through failure of TS
> covariance.

Then pivot:

> That is exactly why I am using Hsu carefully. The case is not "AI solved nonlinear
> quantum mechanics." The case is "AI proposed a technically serious research direction
> that passed peer review and then immediately entered normal post-publication
> scientific dispute." That is, in some ways, the more interesting point.

Use Hsu as evidence for:
