# Tomonaga-Schwinger Covariance and Foliation Notes

## Starting Question

How do we know that the scattering matrix is covariant?

The short answer:

> The S-matrix is covariant when the interaction density transforms covariantly and
> spacelike-separated interaction densities commute, so the result does not depend on
> which Lorentz frame's time-ordering or which spacetime foliation is used.

---

## S-Matrix Covariance in Ordinary QFT

In perturbative QFT, the S-matrix is often written schematically as:

```text
S = T exp[-(i/hbar) integral d^4x H_int(x)]
```

At first sight, this is suspicious because `T` means time-ordering, and different
Lorentz frames can disagree about the time-ordering of spacelike-separated points.

The resolution is **microcausality**:

```text
[H_int(x), H_int(y)] = 0
```

for spacelike-separated `x` and `y`.

For timelike-separated points, all Lorentz frames agree on causal order. For
spacelike-separated points, different frames may reverse the order, but the operators
commute, so the ordering difference has no physical effect.

That is the basic reason the time-ordered exponential can be Lorentz covariant.

Important caveat:

> This is not automatic for every formal theory. Regulators, anomalies, gauge fixing,
> nonlocal interactions, or state-dependent interactions can complicate the proof.
> But in ordinary local relativistic QFT, covariance of the S-matrix follows from
> local covariance plus microcausality.

---

## Tomonaga-Schwinger Version

The Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism expresses the same idea geometrically.

Instead of evolving a state by one preferred global time coordinate:

```text
|Psi(t)> -> |Psi(t + dt)>
```

the state is attached to a spacelike hypersurface:

```text
|Psi[Sigma]>
```

Evolution is generated by local deformations of the hypersurface.

Schematically:

```text
i hbar delta |Psi[Sigma]> / delta sigma(x)
  = H_int(x) |Psi[Sigma]>
```

where:

- `Sigma` is a spacelike hypersurface;
- `x` is a point on it;
- `delta sigma(x)` is a small normal deformation of the hypersurface near `x`;
- `H_int(x)` is the local interaction Hamiltonian density.

The covariance statement becomes:

> If we evolve from an initial spacelike hypersurface to a final spacelike
> hypersurface, the final state should not depend on which intermediate slicing or
> foliation we used.

That is foliation independence.

---

## What "Integrability" Means

Here "integrability" means **path independence**.

In the Tomonaga-Schwinger picture, there are many possible ways to get from an
initial surface `Sigma_i` to a final surface `Sigma_f`.

For example:

```text
Route A: deform near x first, then near y
Route B: deform near y first, then near x
```

If `x` and `y` are spacelike separated, neither order is physically preferred.

The integrability condition requires:

```text
[ delta/delta sigma(x), delta/delta sigma(y) ] |Psi[Sigma]> = 0
```

for spacelike-separated `x` and `y`.

This says:

> deforming near `x` and then near `y` gives the same state as deforming near `y`
> and then near `x`.

In ordinary local QFT, this reduces roughly to:

```text
[H_int(x), H_int(y)] = 0
```

for spacelike-separated points.

That is microcausality.

So:

> The local infinitesimal evolution laws can be integrated up to a finite evolution
> between hypersurfaces only if the result is independent of path or foliation.

If integrability fails, the theory gives local evolution rules but no unique
covariant evolution between hypersurfaces.

---

## Hypersurfaces as Evolution Labels

In the Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism, the spacelike hypersurface replaces global time
as the evolution label.

Ordinary Schrodinger picture:

```text
state: |Psi(t)>
label: time t
```

Tomonaga-Schwinger picture:

```text
state: |Psi[Sigma]>
label: spacelike hypersurface Sigma
```

The physical system does not make the hypersurface evolve. Rather:

> We choose a sequence of spacelike hypersurfaces, and the quantum state is evolved
> from one hypersurface to another.

The hypersurface is a geometric bookkeeping device for relativistic evolution, not a
physical dynamical degree of freedom in ordinary QFT.

---

## Is There a "World Time" Parameter?

Not necessarily.

There is no preferred universal world time in the Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism.

One may choose a one-parameter family of spacelike hypersurfaces:

```text
Sigma_s
```

Then the state can be written:

```text
|Psi[Sigma_s]>
```

and one can say it evolves with respect to `s`.

But `s` is only a **foliation parameter**. It is not a physically preferred time.

Locally, the relevant infinitesimal quantity is:

```text
delta sigma(x)
```

the small normal deformation of the hypersurface at point `x`.

If a foliation `Sigma_s` is chosen, then `delta sigma(x)` is related to `ds` by a
local lapse-like factor:

```text
delta sigma(x) ~ N(x) ds
```

where `N(x)` tells how much the surface advances normally at point `x` as `s`
changes.

---

## Family of Possible Parameters and Foliations

There are two levels of freedom.

### 1. Reparameterizing the Same Foliation

Suppose we have a fixed family of hypersurfaces:

```text
Sigma_s
```

We can relabel the same surfaces by another monotonic parameter:

```text
s' = f(s)
```

This changes only the labels, not the hypersurfaces.

### 2. Choosing a Different Foliation

More importantly, we can choose a genuinely different slicing:

```text
Sigma_s
```

versus:

```text
Sigma'_{s'}
```

These are not merely relabelings. They are different families of spacelike
hypersurfaces connecting the same initial and final regions.

One foliation might use flat constant-time slices in one Lorentz frame. Another
might use tilted or curved spacelike surfaces, or surfaces that advance faster in one
spatial region than another.

The Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism is designed to allow all such choices.

The transformations involved are broader than:

```text
s -> f(s)
```

They include changes of slicing, often described in canonical relativity language by
lapse and shift:

- **lapse**: how far the hypersurface advances normally at each point;
- **shift**: how coordinates slide along the hypersurface.

Hsu's discussion focuses on normal deformations:

```text
delta sigma(x)
```

The integrability condition says these local normal deformations must fit together
consistently.

---

## Continuous Nature of Foliations

In the usual Tomonaga-Schwinger setup, a foliation is taken to vary continuously and
smoothly.

One imagines a one-parameter family:

```text
Sigma_s
```

where `s` changes continuously.

Neighboring surfaces:

```text
Sigma_s
Sigma_{s + ds}
```

differ by infinitesimal local deformations.

Each step can be nonuniform across space: one region of the surface may advance more
than another. The local displacement is encoded in:

```text
delta sigma(x)
```

The formalism is differential, so it assumes smooth motion through the space of
spacelike hypersurfaces. For calculations, one may approximate this by a sequence of
small discrete deformations and then take a continuum limit.

---

## Local Covariance Versus Full Foliation Independence

A spacelike hypersurface is a coordinate-independent geometric object. Different
observers may assign different coordinates to it, but the surface itself is geometric.

The Tomonaga-Schwinger equation is locally covariant because it is written in terms
of:

- spacetime points `x`;
- local hypersurface deformations `delta sigma(x)`;
- local Hamiltonian densities `H_int(x)`;
- no preferred global inertial time coordinate.

But local covariance is not enough.

You also need the local deformations to fit together consistently into a unique
finite evolution between hypersurfaces.

That is the integrability condition.

Clean distinction:

1. **Local covariance**:
   The equation is written using spacetime points, hypersurface deformations, and
   local covariant densities rather than a preferred global time.

2. **Foliation independence / integrability**:
   The local covariant evolution rules produce the same final state regardless of the
   slicing used.

For ordinary local QFT, microcausality supplies the second condition.

---

## Compact Talk Version

> The S-matrix is covariant in ordinary local QFT because the interaction density is
> local and covariant, and spacelike-separated interaction densities commute. In
> Tomonaga-Schwinger language, this becomes foliation independence: the state is
> assigned to an arbitrary spacelike hypersurface, and evolving it by local
> deformations should give the same final state no matter how the spacetime region is
> sliced. The "integrability" condition is exactly this path-independence condition.

---

## One-Line Version

> Tomonaga-Schwinger integrability means that local covariant evolution rules can be
> integrated from one spacelike hypersurface to another without the answer depending
> on the chosen foliation.
