# Weinberg-Style Nonlinear Quantum Mechanics Summary

## What It Means

"Weinberg-style nonlinear quantum mechanics" refers to Steven Weinberg's 1989
proposal for modifying ordinary quantum mechanics by allowing the Schrödinger
evolution to be nonlinear while preserving as much of the standard formal structure
as possible.

In ordinary quantum mechanics, the state vector evolves linearly:

```text
i d|psi>/dt = H |psi>
```

The Hamiltonian `H` is a linear operator. If `|psi_1>` and `|psi_2>` are possible
states, then superpositions evolve consistently because the equation is linear.

Weinberg asked: what if the evolution law is slightly nonlinear in the state, but
still respects core structural requirements such as phase invariance, norm
preservation, and a Hamiltonian-like formulation?

---

## Weinberg's Formal Move

Instead of treating observables as linear Hermitian operators, Weinberg generalized
observables to real-valued functions on Hilbert space.

The Hamiltonian becomes a function `h(psi, psi*)`, homogeneous in the state variables,
and the equation of motion is generated by derivatives of this function. Schematically:

```text
i d psi_j / dt = partial h / partial psi*_j
```

If `h` is quadratic in `psi` and `psi*`, this reduces to ordinary linear quantum
mechanics. If `h` contains higher-order dependence on the state, the dynamics becomes
nonlinear.

The point was not to overthrow quantum mechanics, but to create a controlled
phenomenological framework for testing whether quantum mechanics is exactly linear.
Weinberg wanted a formalism in which one could write possible nonlinear corrections,
derive observable consequences, and compare with experiment.

---

## Why It Is Dangerous

The problem is that nonlinear evolution interacts badly with entanglement.

In ordinary quantum mechanics, Bob's local statistics depend only on his reduced
density matrix, not on how that density matrix is decomposed into possible pure-state
ensembles.

In a nonlinear theory, different decompositions of the same density matrix can evolve
differently. If Alice and Bob share an entangled state, Alice's measurement choice can
remotely prepare different ensemble decompositions for Bob. Bob's nonlinear dynamics
can then distinguish those decompositions.

That creates a superluminal signaling channel.

This is the point sharpened by Gisin and Polchinski: Weinberg-style nonlinear quantum
mechanics is not just "ordinary quantum mechanics plus small nonlinear corrections."
Once combined with entanglement, it threatens the no-signaling structure that makes
ordinary quantum mechanics compatible with relativity.

---

## Relation to Polchinski

Polchinski's 1991 result is the standard objection.

He showed that Weinberg-style nonlinear quantum mechanics faces a dilemma:

- If the nonlinear evolution acts naturally on pure states, EPR correlations become
  usable for superluminal signaling.
- If one tries to avoid that by making subsystem evolution depend only on reduced
  density matrices, one gets the strange alternative of communication between
  branches of the wavefunction, the "Everett phone."

So the issue is not merely that a particular nonlinear correction has an experimental
constraint. The issue is structural: nonlinearity changes how mixtures and entangled
subsystems evolve, and that threatens the operational separation between distant
systems.

---

## Relation to Hsu

Hsu's paper asks whether this kind of state-dependent nonlinear modification can be
made compatible with relativistic QFT using the Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism.

The Hsu/Diosi dispute is about where the obstruction lies:

- Hsu argues that state-dependent nonlinear QFT evolution generically violates
  Tomonaga-Schwinger foliation independence, hence relativistic covariance.
- Diosi argues that the Tomonaga-Schwinger equation can remain formally covariant,
  while the causality problem remains an operational signaling problem of the
  Gisin/Polchinski type.

Either way, Weinberg-style nonlinear quantum mechanics is the background framework:
it is the controlled nonlinear deformation of quantum mechanics whose compatibility
with entanglement, signaling, and relativity is under pressure.

## Talk Version

> Weinberg-style nonlinear quantum mechanics is a controlled attempt to generalize
> Schrödinger evolution by allowing the Hamiltonian to depend nonlinearly on the
> quantum state. It preserves much of the formal Hamiltonian structure of quantum
> mechanics, but Polchinski showed that, when applied to entangled systems, it
> generically permits either superluminal signaling or other exotic pathologies such
> as communication between branches of the wavefunction.

---

## One-Line Version

> Weinberg-style nonlinear QM keeps the Hamiltonian framework but lets the Hamiltonian
> depend nonlinearly on the state; the price is that entanglement can become a
> signaling channel.
