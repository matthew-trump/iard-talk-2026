## What it is

A 4-page peer-reviewed paper in *Physics Letters B*. A no-go theorem in quantum
foundations: state-dependent nonlinear modifications to QFT are incompatible with
relativistic covariance under the Tomonaga-Schwinger formalism.

---

## The physics

**The question**: Can you modify QM to be nonlinear (state-dependent) while preserving
relativistic covariance? Linearity of QM is exact experimentally, but theorists
have long asked whether small nonlinear corrections are possible in principle.
Prior work (Polchinski 1991) showed nonlinearity causes superluminal signaling — a
fatal objection in relativistic theory. This paper settles the question at the QFT
level.

**The tool**: Tomonaga-Schwinger (TS) formalism. Defines QFT evolution on arbitrary
spacelike hypersurfaces rather than a fixed time coordinate. For a covariant theory,
physics must be independent of which foliation of spacetime you choose — this is the
TS integrability condition.

**The result**: State-dependent nonlinear modifications generically fail the TS
integrability condition. Key mechanism: state-dependent evolution does not preserve
microcausality (commutativity of spacelike-separated operators) when propagated
forward from an initial Cauchy slice. Three models analyzed and all fail:
- Weinberg local expectation-value form (1989)
- Weinberg nonlocal mean-field generalization
- Kaplan-Rajendran retarded model (2022)

**The conclusion**: Under TS integrability with microcausality, admissible
deterministic state-dependent modifications collapse to the linear case.
