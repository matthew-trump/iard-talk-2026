## What it is

A position paper — structured argument about what LLMs currently can and cannot do
in theoretical physics, and what would be needed to make them useful. Written by a
collaboration including the Cirac group at MPQ (central to quantum many-body and
quantum information) and Schölkopf's group at MPI-IS (a leading ML lab). Unusual
credibility on both sides.

**Central position**:
> *"LLM agents, when appropriately adapted and integrated with domain-specialized
> training and tooling, could potentially serve as a promising technology for
> accelerating discovery in theoretical physics — provided their current limitations
> in rigorous reasoning, physical grounding, and reliability are systematically
> addressed through targeted interdisciplinary research."*

## Skill analysis

**Mathematical/symbolic reasoning**: Errors in algebra and calculus despite strong
benchmark performance. Small errors compound in long derivations. Unit consistency
particularly fragile (mixing SI and natural units). Path integrals problematic.
Benchmark performance ≠ derivation reliability.

**Physical consistency**: LLMs can generate "mathematically plausible but physically
violate fundamental laws if not carefully guided." When faced with ambiguous problems,
"LLMs tend to randomly pick one path without justification." Example failure:
placing system spins and bath spins on the same lattice sites.

**Approximation judgment**: May apply perturbation theory without stating validity
condition |λ⟨ψ_m|V|ψ_n⟩| ≪ |E_m − E_n|. Identifying the right approximation
scheme is physical judgment not encoded in formal rules.

**Research taste**: May opt for brute-force over elegant approaches. Example:
computing ⟨x̂⟩_n by integration rather than noting x is odd, |ψ_n|² is even,
integral of (odd × even) over symmetric interval is zero by symmetry.

**Code generation**: Most reliable skill. But physics-aware issues remain: missing
fermionic anticommutation rules, failing to enforce Gauss's law in lattice gauge
implementations.

## The Chern insulator example

Diagonalizing H(k) = d(k)·σ is trivial linear algebra. But the physics is in the
winding of d(k) in momentum space — a topological invariant determining quantized
Hall conductivity. An LLM that executes the linear algebra may miss the physics
entirely. Knowing to look for the winding number is not derivable from the formalism.

## The characteristic failure pattern

> *"Models correctly execute standard procedures (e.g., routine operator expansions)
> but fail at the key non-standard step requiring genuine insight — discovering a
> novel proof technique or recognizing a technically delicate prerequisite before
> the main argument can proceed."*

Failure clusters at the pivot point. This explains why the Pan et al. HF template
works: it eliminates the pivot point from GPT-4's task by encoding it in the template.

---

## The Deriver-Critic loop

> *"One agent generates derivations while another critiques them — either
> autonomously or with a human expert validating the critic's annotations.
> Crucially, critic agents should operate without access to intermediate
> generation artifacts to ensure independent verification."*

Convergent independent discovery with Hsu's Generate-Verify protocol and Pan et al.'s
sequential correction — same principle, different implementation.

---

## The de-skilling concern and Mathematica analogy

"Depending too heavily on LLMs for tasks like mathematical derivations could risk
degrading these essential skills among physicists, especially those in training.
Deep intuition often arises from performing detailed calculations firsthand."

The Mathematica analogy: "tools like Mathematica initially raised de-skilling concerns
but ultimately enabled mathematicians to focus on higher-level work."

---

## The communicability requirement

> *"Agents should mirror how theoretical physics groups operate: presenting results at
> varying levels of detail, highlighting why certain approaches work, and enabling
> supervisors to probe assumptions — so that discoveries can be understood, critiqued,
> and built upon. If agents merely produce piles of derivations without conveying the
> discovery process and key insights, humans are reduced to mechanical checking rather
> than scientific understanding."*

The physics analog of Tao's proof indigestion.

---

## The Lu/Hsu tension

Lu et al. conclude LLMs are "inadequate" without specialized training. Hsu, in the
same period, published a peer-reviewed QFT paper with general-purpose LLMs and no
specialized training. Resolution: the expert bottleneck. Hsu's expertise was what
allowed him to recognize the TS suggestion as correct and reject Reeh-Schlieder.
Lu et al. characterizes what goes wrong *without* the expert; Hsu shows what's
possible *with* the expert.
